# Event-contamination research coverage

Research conducted 2026-09-12 using public web search and accessible primary/news sources. The [CSV](../../config/event_contamination.csv) contains 51 event-linked records, including duplicate links where the same news belongs to both film windows. Dates are public announcement/reporting dates unless the notes state otherwise. Severity is an analyst judgment of plausible relevance, **not an estimate of price impact**.

The January window crosses New Year's Day and MLK Day; the hearing window crosses Juneteenth. Weekend/holiday news is retained because it can affect the next trading session. The CSV is a qualitative log, not a programmatic event-day reassignment or a filter on the return panel.

Queries combined Boeing with date ranges and dated weekday searches; specific topics included FAA inspections/grounding, manufacturing and deliveries, Senate whistleblowers, DOJ developments, Spirit AeroSystems, Starliner, contracts, divestments and labor. Sources searched included Boeing investor/newsroom releases, FAA dated updates, NASA's June archive, Senate releases and Reuters/AP reports, sometimes available through syndication. This covers the required search ranges but **does not establish an exhaustive historical news census**.

## Interpretation supported by the log

- **Alaska:** weekend grounding is already part of the information available on the first tradable event day. Broader inspection findings, regulatory investigation and production oversight followed. Annual orders/deliveries were also disclosed. Longer losses bundle these developments and cannot be assigned solely to the initial accident.
- **Hearing:** advance testimony and same-day whistleblower allegations are related event information. Manufacturing disclosures, titanium documentation concerns, Starliner delays, legal reporting and Spirit acquisition coverage are additional possible drivers. The hearing was not an isolated information shock.
- **Theatrical sensitivity:** the range includes divestment and monthly-delivery disclosures before release, plus defense frameworks and subsequent streaming exposure.
- **Streaming:** the range includes theatrical exposure, contract/framework news and labor developments. A positive defense contract announcement and negative labor news could offset each other or the film's response; this study does not separately identify their effects.

These are reasons to qualify causal language, not grounds to delete dates after examining returns. The film's description includes potential new revelations. Neither this research nor the return model establishes that investors already knew everything in it.

## Timing and access limitations

Some articles appeared after the stock-market close, and syndication can obscure the original publication time. The notes distinguish release dates, reporting dates and underlying event dates where available. Do not silently assign an evening announcement to the preceding closing return. In particular, the August 26 contractor-recruitment report was in the evening: August 25 job postings may have been visible earlier, but first market awareness is unverified, and the response to the report may fall on August 27 outside the streaming ±5 range.

Reuters's August 14 Wisk analysis was accessible only as metadata and opening text; its row is limited to that supported scope. The possible Qatar aircraft sale is supported by a dated reproduction of a State Department release; the direct government page did not load. Its amount is an authorization ceiling for a broader possible package, not a completed Boeing sale. The F-15 award likewise has a contract ceiling rather than guaranteed revenue. Strike authorization does not mean a strike began.

No entry should be read as a conclusion of misconduct unless the underlying source establishes it. Senate material concerns allegations; titanium documentation concerns do not prove aircraft material was unsafe; investigation of an aircraft event does not by itself establish a manufacturing defect.

Unconfirmed leads (including proposed 787 nacelle changes, Vietnam Airlines incident coverage and assorted small awards/analyst changes) were not promoted into factual rows. A routine January 4 earnings-date announcement was judged immaterial and omitted. Dates without rows must not be called free of confounding news.

The January 24 production-expansion cap and June 27 NTSB sanctions are outside the mandatory ±5 windows. An August 28 report of an August 24 credit agreement is not treated as information available on August 24 without contemporaneous evidence. Longer +10 return windows exceed the researched ±5 boundaries and therefore carry additional unlogged-news uncertainty.

The source log and these notes are manually researched provenance. Re-running the numerical pipeline reads them locally; it does not rerun web searches or verify that remote pages remain unchanged.
